Made to Play Longer

A way for Palmes to talk about sustainability without pretending to be something it is not. Fewer pieces, better materials, and facts instead of green symbols.

ClientPalmes Tennis Society
ContextFinal exam project, KEA 2026
ServicesBrand analysis, Concept, Visual direction, Prototypes
MethodsDesign Thinking, Aaker, SWOT, interviews, survey
The brief

Palmes is a Copenhagen tennis brand with a strong universe: red clay, court lines and a love of the game. Sustainability was never part of the story. As the founder put it in our interview, it simply wasn't what Palmes was built around. The question was how Palmes could take responsibility in a way that felt true to the brand, instead of bolting on a green layer.

What I found

I analysed how Aiayu, GANNI and Fichi communicate sustainability, and combined it with a survey and interviews with people in the target group. Everything pointed the same way. To be believed, the message has to be simple, documentable and rooted in the brand's own identity. Big promises and leaves on the swing tag do the opposite.

The concept

Made to Play Longer turns limits into the story. Each piece is made in a small, numbered run, often from deadstock fabric left over from earlier production. 140 pieces, because that is how much fabric there was. The tone is concrete and a little dry: “We're not there yet. But here is what we do.”

Design choices

Tennis court lines work as the graphic system, so the sustainability layer looks like Palmes and not like every other eco brand. Material facts sit in a clear table on the product page and on the back of the hangtag, with a QR code leading to the full story. No globes, no leaves, no “100% sustainable”.
